1. How to Learn to Draw:

Avoid rote learning. In the context of drawing, this is the mistake of simply memorising the outputs of what you draw without any foundational/deep understanding of your subject, such as their underlying shape and proportions.

If this habit is developed, then you become unable to draw flexibly—you can only draw subjects in the same way that you've memorised them, e.g. being unable to portray different angles/perspective. In a sense, it's as if you are drawing by "placing down" stickers, instead of having a three-dimensional understanding of an object in your head and transferring it to the medium. For example, a human eye; learning how to draw it by simply copying a front-facing reference of it, simply memorising that output, and then using only that output to draw eyes. This isn't to say that you should just memorise many outputs, that is again simply rote learning and you wouldn't be developing any understanding of what it is that you're drawing.

So then how do you develop an understanding of a subject?

Analyse its shape and proportions.

These two concepts are the fundamental building blocks to learning how to draw. You learn to draw by seeing, analysing, and understanding these two things; the physical motion of drawing is only a percentage of it.
